# Key Terms & Concepts — Nutrition

A curated, scannable reference of important terms in nutrition. Hover a term to reveal its
definition, aliases, and an external anchor. Terms are grouped by real sub-topics — never a
`General` or `Other` catch-all.

## Macronutrients

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Carbohydrate (1)
- Protein (2)
- Fat (3)
- Dietary fiber (4)

</div>

1.  A macronutrient composed of sugars, starches, and fibers, and one of the body's primary sources of energy. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbohydrate).
2.  A macronutrient made of amino acids, essential for building and repairing tissues, enzymes, and hormones. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_(nutrient)).
3.  An energy-dense macronutrient (about 9 kcal per gram) that stores energy, cushions organs, and enables the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins. **Aliases:** lipid. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat).
4.  The indigestible portion of plant foods that aids digestion, regulates blood sugar, and supports gut health. **Aliases:** roughage. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietary_fiber).

## Micronutrients

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Vitamin (1)
- Mineral (2)
- Antioxidant (3)

</div>

1.  An organic compound required in small amounts for normal metabolism that the body generally cannot synthesize in sufficient quantity. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin).
2.  An inorganic chemical element required by the body in small amounts for functions such as bone structure, nerve signaling, and fluid balance. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_(nutrient)).
3.  A compound that inhibits oxidation and can neutralize free radicals, potentially reducing cellular damage. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioxidant).

## Grains & Cereals

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Cereal (1)
- Wheat (2)
- Spelt (3)
- Rye (4)
- Whole grain (5)
- Gluten (6)

</div>

1.  A grass cultivated for the edible starch of its grain; staple cereals include wheat, rice, maize, barley, oats, and rye. **Aliases:** cereal grain. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereal).
2.  A cereal grain of the genus *Triticum*, one of the world's most widely grown staple crops and a primary source of dietary starch and gluten. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat).
3.  An ancient species of wheat (*Triticum spelta*) cultivated since roughly 5000 BC, valued as a hardy grain with a nutty flavor. **Aliases:** dinkel wheat, hulled wheat. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spelt).
4.  A cereal grain (*Secale cereale*) related to wheat and barley, widely used for bread, whiskey, and animal fodder and tolerant of poor soils. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye).
5.  A grain that retains all three parts of the intact kernel — the bran, germ, and endosperm — and therefore more fiber and nutrients than refined grain. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_grain).
6.  A family of storage proteins found in wheat and related grains that gives dough its elasticity and triggers coeliac disease in susceptible people. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gluten).

## Dietary Patterns

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Paleo diet (1)
- Mediterranean diet (2)
- Ketogenic diet (3)
- Veganism (4)

</div>

1.  A modern dietary pattern based on foods presumed to have been eaten during the Paleolithic era — meats, fish, vegetables, fruits, nuts, and seeds — while excluding grains, legumes, and dairy. **Aliases:** paleolithic diet, caveman diet. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic_diet).
2.  A dietary pattern inspired by the eating habits of Mediterranean countries, emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, olive oil, and fish. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_diet).
3.  A high-fat, adequate-protein, very-low-carbohydrate diet that shifts the body's metabolism toward burning fat for fuel (ketosis). **Aliases:** keto diet. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketogenic_diet).
4.  A practice of abstaining from the use of animal products, particularly in the diet, relying entirely on plant-based foods. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veganism).

## Metabolism & Energy

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Calorie (1)
- Metabolism (2)
- Basal metabolic rate (3)
- Glycemic index (4)

</div>

1.  A unit of energy; in nutrition, the "calorie" on food labels is the kilocalorie, the energy needed to raise one kilogram of water by one degree Celsius. **Aliases:** kilocalorie, kcal. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calorie).
2.  The set of life-sustaining chemical reactions that break food down for energy (catabolism) and build cellular components (anabolism). **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism).
3.  The rate of energy expenditure per unit time by an organism at rest, used to estimate baseline calorie needs. **Aliases:** BMR. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_metabolic_rate).
4.  A ranking of carbohydrate-containing foods by how much they raise blood glucose relative to a reference food. **Aliases:** GI. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycemic_index).
