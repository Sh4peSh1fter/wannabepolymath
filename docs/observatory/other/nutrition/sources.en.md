# Sources & Further Reading — Nutrition

A curated list of resources for diving deeper into nutrition.

## Websites & Online Publications

1. The Nutrition Source — Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health
    - *Link*: [nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/)
    - *Note*: Evidence-based nutrition guidance from Harvard's Department of Nutrition, covering food groups, dietary patterns, and healthy eating plates.
2. WHO — Healthy Diet
    - *Link*: [who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)
    - *Note*: The World Health Organization's global fact sheet on the components of a healthy diet.
3. MyPlate — U.S. Department of Agriculture
    - *Link*: [myplate.gov](https://www.myplate.gov/)
    - *Note*: The USDA's practical guide to building balanced meals under the U.S. Dietary Guidelines.
4. NutritionFacts.org
    - *Link*: [nutritionfacts.org](https://nutritionfacts.org/)
    - *Note*: A non-commercial site summarizing peer-reviewed nutrition research, run by physician Michael Greger.

## Videos & Documentaries

1. *Forks Over Knives* (2011)
    - *Link*: [forksoverknives.com](https://www.forksoverknives.com/)
    - *Note*: A documentary examining the claim that a whole-food, plant-based diet can prevent or reverse chronic disease.

## Academic Papers & Journals

1. The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition
    - *Link*: [ajcn.nutrition.org](https://ajcn.nutrition.org/)
    - *Note*: A leading peer-reviewed journal publishing original research across human nutrition.

## Books

1. *In Defense of Food: An Eater's Manifesto* — by Michael Pollan
    - *Note*: A widely read argument for eating whole, minimally processed foods, summarized as "Eat food. Not too much. Mostly plants."
2. *How Not to Die* — by Michael Greger
    - *Note*: A survey of the dietary evidence around leading causes of death, from the founder of NutritionFacts.org.
