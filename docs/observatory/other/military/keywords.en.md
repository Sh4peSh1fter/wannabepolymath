# Key Terms & Concepts — Military

A curated, scannable reference of important terms in the military field. Hover a term to reveal
its definition, aliases, and an external anchor. Terms are grouped by real sub-topics — never a
`General` or `Other` catch-all.

## Strategy & Doctrine

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Strategy (1)
- Tactics (2)
- Military doctrine (3)
- Deterrence (4)
- Attrition warfare (5)
- Maneuver warfare (6)
- Asymmetric warfare (7)
- Combined arms (8)

</div>

1.  The planning and coordination of military resources to achieve overarching political or wartime objectives. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_strategy).
2.  The organization and use of forces in and around actual engagements — the conduct of individual battles. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tactics).
3.  The set of fundamental principles guiding how military forces act in support of objectives. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_doctrine).
4.  The use of threats — especially of retaliation — to dissuade an adversary from taking an unwanted action. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterrence_theory).
5.  A strategy of wearing down the enemy through continuous losses in personnel and materiel. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attrition_warfare).
6.  A strategy that seeks to defeat the enemy by shattering their cohesion and decision-making rather than through attrition. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maneuver_warfare).
7.  Conflict between belligerents whose relative military power, strategy, or tactics differ significantly. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymmetric_warfare).
8.  The coordinated use of different arms — such as infantry, armour, artillery, and air power — to achieve complementary effects. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_arms).

## Military Organization

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Military branch (1)
- Division (2)
- Battalion (3)
- Command and control (4)
- Chain of command (5)

</div>

1.  A distinct sub-organization of a state's armed forces, such as an army, navy, or air force. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch).
2.  A large military unit, typically composed of several brigades or regiments and numbering in the thousands. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_(military)).
3.  A military unit usually consisting of several companies, commanded by a lieutenant colonel. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion).
4.  The exercise of authority by a commander over assigned forces to accomplish a mission. **Aliases:** C2. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_and_control).
5.  The line of authority and responsibility along which orders pass within a military organization. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_hierarchy).

## Weapons & Systems

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Artillery (1)
- Tank (2)
- Ballistic missile (3)
- Unmanned aerial vehicle (4)
- Nuclear weapon (5)

</div>

1.  Ranged weapons that launch munitions far beyond the range of infantry weapons, including guns, howitzers, and rockets. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery).
2.  An armoured fighting vehicle designed for front-line combat, combining heavy firepower, protection, and mobility. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank).
3.  A missile that follows a ballistic trajectory to deliver a warhead to a predetermined target. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile).
4.  An aircraft without a human pilot on board, operated remotely or autonomously. **Aliases:** UAV, drone. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_aerial_vehicle).
5.  An explosive device deriving destructive force from nuclear fission or fusion reactions. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon).

## Logistics

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Military logistics (1)
- Line of communication (2)
- Materiel (3)
- Mobilization (4)

</div>

1.  The discipline of planning and carrying out the movement, supply, and maintenance of military forces. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_logistics).
2.  A route connecting an operating military unit with its supply base, along which supplies and reinforcements move. **Aliases:** LOC. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_communication).
3.  The equipment, supplies, and weapons used by an armed force. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materiel).
4.  The act of assembling and preparing troops and supplies for war or other emergencies. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobilization).

## Intelligence

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Military intelligence (1)
- Reconnaissance (2)
- Signals intelligence (3)
- Human intelligence (4)

</div>

1.  The collection, analysis, and dissemination of information relevant to military planning and operations. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_intelligence).
2.  Exploration outside an area occupied by friendly forces to gain information about the enemy or terrain. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconnaissance).
3.  Intelligence gathered by intercepting signals, whether communications or electronic emissions. **Aliases:** SIGINT. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence).
4.  Intelligence gathered from human sources through interpersonal contact. **Aliases:** HUMINT. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_intelligence_(intelligence_gathering)).

## Warfare Types & Domains

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Guerrilla warfare (1)
- Siege (2)
- Total war (3)
- Cyberwarfare (4)

</div>

1.  Irregular warfare in which small groups use mobility, ambushes, and raids to fight a larger, less mobile force. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla_warfare).
2.  A military blockade of a fortified place, coupled with assaults, to compel its surrender. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege).
3.  Warfare in which a nation mobilizes all of its resources and disregards the usual limits on legitimate targets. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_war).
4.  The use of digital attacks to damage, disrupt, or gain advantage over an adversary's systems and infrastructure. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare).
