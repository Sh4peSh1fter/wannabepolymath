# Military

## Introduction

A military — collectively, the armed forces — is a heavily armed, highly organized force
primarily intended for warfare, typically authorized and maintained by a sovereign state. The
field spans the enduring questions of **strategy and doctrine** (how force is planned and
applied), the **organization** that puts it into practice, the **weapons and systems** it
wields, and the **logistics** and **intelligence** that sustain and inform it. This page is a
**Map of Content**: the definitions live in `keywords`, the people and institutions in
`entities`, and the reading in `sources`.

## Related concepts & fields

- **[History](../../social_sciences/history/index.en.md)** — wars and campaigns are central to
  historical narrative, and military history is where strategy and doctrine are tested and
  studied.
- **[Political Science](../../social_sciences/political_science/index.en.md)** — the military is
  an instrument of the state; deterrence, alliances (NATO), and civil-military relations sit at
  the intersection of the two fields.
- **[Engineering](../../applied_sciences/engineering/index.en.md)** — weapons, vehicles, and
  systems (artillery, tanks, missiles, UAVs) are products of applied engineering and drive much
  of its innovation.
