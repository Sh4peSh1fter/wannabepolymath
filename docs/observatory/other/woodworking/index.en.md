# Woodworking

## Introduction

Woodworking is the craft and skill of making things from wood — furniture, cabinetry,
carvings, and turned objects. It sits where an understanding of the material (how wood grows,
dries, and moves) meets a repertoire of joints, hand and power tools, and finishing
techniques. This field is organized in three views: the working **vocabulary**, the **people
and organizations** behind it, and where to **read further**. Newcomers usually reach first
for the core joints ([mortise and tenon](./keywords.en.md#joinery),
[dovetail](./keywords.en.md#joinery)), a small kit of [hand tools](./keywords.en.md#hand-tools),
and the basics of [wood as a material](./keywords.en.md#wood-materials).

## Related concepts & fields

- **[Engineering](../../applied_sciences/engineering/index.en.md)** — shares the study of
  materials and structures; joints, loads, and the strength of wood are engineering questions
  applied at the workbench.
- **[Chemistry](../../natural_sciences/chemistry/index.en.md)** — finishes, adhesives, and
  solvents (varnish, lacquer, shellac, wood glue) are applied chemistry that determines how a
  surface protects and ages.
- **[Biology](../../natural_sciences/biology/index.en.md)** — wood is a biological material;
  how trees grow explains grain, hardwood versus softwood, and how a board moves with moisture.
