# Key Terms & Concepts — Woodworking

A curated, scannable reference of important terms in woodworking. Hover a term to reveal its
definition, aliases, and an external anchor. Terms are grouped by real sub-topics — never a
`General` or `Other` catch-all.

## Joinery

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Mortise and tenon (1)
- Dovetail joint (2)
- Finger joint (3)
- Dowel (4)
- Biscuit joint (5)

</div>

1.  A joint in which a projecting tenon on one piece fits a matching mortise (cavity) cut into another; among the oldest and strongest woodworking joints. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortise_and_tenon).
2.  An interlocking joint of trapezoidal tails and pins that resists being pulled apart, prized for drawers and casework. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dovetail_joint).
3.  A joint made from complementary interlocking rectangular profiles cut into two pieces. **Aliases:** box joint, comb joint. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger_joint).
4.  A cylindrical wooden pin used to align and reinforce a joint between two pieces. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dowel).
5.  A joint made by gluing a compressed oval biscuit into matching crescent slots cut in the mating pieces to align them. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biscuit_joiner).

## Hand Tools

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Chisel (1)
- Hand plane (2)
- Hand saw (3)
- Mallet (4)
- Clamp (5)

</div>

1.  A hand tool with a sharpened edge on its end, driven by hand or mallet to cut or pare wood. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chisel).
2.  A tool that flattens, smooths, or shapes wood by shaving thin curls with a fixed blade. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_(tool)).
3.  A tool that cuts wood with a toothed blade worked by hand. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saw).
4.  A hammer with a large, usually wooden head, used to drive chisels or assemble joints without marring the work. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallet).
5.  A fastening device that holds workpieces firmly together, especially while glue cures. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clamp_(tool)).

## Power Tools

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Table saw (1)
- Bandsaw (2)
- Router (3)
- Jointer (4)
- Thickness planer (5)
- Lathe (6)

</div>

1.  A saw with a circular blade projecting through a flat table, used for straight rip and cross cuts. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_saw).
2.  A saw with a continuous toothed band running over wheels, used for curved cuts and resawing. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandsaw).
3.  A power tool that hollows out or shapes wood with a spinning bit, used for edges, grooves, and joinery. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Router_(woodworking)).
4.  A machine that flattens one face and squares one edge of a board to create a reference surface. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jointer).
5.  A machine that trims a board to a uniform thickness parallel to its reference face. **Aliases:** planer, thicknesser. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thickness_planer).
6.  A machine that rotates a workpiece on its axis so tools can shape it symmetrically; central to woodturning. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lathe).

## Wood & Materials

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Hardwood (1)
- Softwood (2)
- Lumber (3)
- Plywood (4)
- Medium-density fibreboard (5)
- Wood veneer (6)
- Wood grain (7)

</div>

1.  Wood from angiosperm (broad-leaved) trees such as oak, maple, and walnut. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardwood).
2.  Wood from gymnosperm (conifer) trees such as pine, spruce, and fir. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softwood).
3.  Wood processed into beams and boards for construction and woodworking. **Aliases:** timber. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumber).
4.  An engineered sheet material made of thin wood veneers glued with alternating grain directions for strength. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plywood).
5.  An engineered panel made by bonding wood fibres under heat and pressure. **Aliases:** MDF. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium-density_fibreboard).
6.  Thin slices of wood glued onto a core panel to give the appearance of solid wood. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_veneer).
7.  The longitudinal arrangement and appearance of wood fibres, which affects strength, workability, and finish. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_grain).

## Finishing

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Wood finishing (1)
- Varnish (2)
- Lacquer (3)
- Shellac (4)
- Danish oil (5)
- Wood stain (6)

</div>

1.  The processes of refining and protecting a wood surface, from sanding through the application of a finish. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_finishing).
2.  A hard, transparent protective finish, typically a resin dissolved in oil or solvent. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varnish).
3.  A fast-drying solvent-based finish that forms a hard film. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacquer).
4.  A natural resin secreted by the lac insect, dissolved in alcohol and used as a finish. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellac).
5.  A penetrating oil finish, often an oil-and-varnish blend, that hardens within the wood. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_oil).
6.  A colorant used to change or enhance the color of wood while letting the grain show through. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_stain).

## Techniques & Operations

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Wood carving (1)
- Woodturning (2)
- Wood drying (3)

</div>

1.  Shaping wood by cutting with tools such as knives, chisels, and gouges. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_carving).
2.  Shaping wood on a lathe with handheld tools to make symmetrical, rounded objects. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodturning).
3.  Reducing the moisture content of wood, by air drying or kiln drying, to improve stability before use. **Aliases:** seasoning. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_drying).
