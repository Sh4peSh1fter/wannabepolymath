# Earth Science

## Introduction

Earth science studies our planet's physical systems — from earthquakes to raindrops, from the
deep inner core to the outer atmosphere. Its sub-fields hang together because they trace the
same matter and energy through different reservoirs: **geology** reads the solid rock record,
**plate tectonics** explains how that rock moves and reshapes continents, **meteorology** and
**climatology** govern the atmosphere on short and long timescales, and **oceanography** and
**hydrology** follow water through seas, ground, and sky. A newcomer should anchor first on
plate tectonics and the rock cycle (the solid Earth) and the water and carbon cycles (its
fluid envelopes) — most other concepts connect back to these. This page is a Map of Content:
the definitions live in `keywords`, the people and institutions in `entities`, and the reading
in `sources`.

## Related concepts & fields

- **[Physics](../physics/index.en.md)** — supplies the mechanics, thermodynamics, and wave
  theory behind seismic waves, atmospheric circulation, and ocean dynamics.
- **[Chemistry](../chemistry/index.en.md)** — underpins mineralogy, the composition of rocks
  and seawater, and the chemistry of the atmosphere and greenhouse gases.
- **[Astronomy](../astronomy/index.en.md)** — places Earth among the planets; orbital and
  solar variation (Milankovitch cycles) links planetary motion to Earth's climate.
- **[Biology](../biology/index.en.md)** — meets Earth science in the fossil record,
  biogeochemical cycles, and the co-evolution of life and the planet's surface.
