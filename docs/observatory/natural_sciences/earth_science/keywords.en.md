# Key Terms & Concepts — Earth Science

A curated, scannable reference of important terms in Earth science. Hover a term to reveal its
definition, aliases, and an external anchor. Terms are grouped by real sub-fields — never a
`General` or `Other` catch-all.

## Geology & Earth Materials

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Geology (1)
- Mineral (2)
- Rock cycle (3)
- Igneous rock (4)
- Sedimentary rock (5)
- Metamorphic rock (6)

</div>

1.  The science of the solid Earth — the rocks it is made of and the processes by which they change over time. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology).
2.  A naturally occurring inorganic solid with a definite chemical composition and an ordered crystalline structure. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral).
3.  The set of transitions through geologic time among the three main rock types, driven by weathering, melting, and lithification. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_cycle).
4.  Rock formed through the cooling and solidification of magma or lava. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igneous_rock).
5.  Rock formed by the accumulation, compaction, and cementation of mineral or organic sediment. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary_rock).
6.  Rock transformed from a pre-existing rock type by heat, pressure, and chemically active fluids. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamorphic_rock).

## Plate Tectonics & the Solid Earth

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Plate tectonics (1)
- Continental drift (2)
- Seafloor spreading (3)
- Subduction (4)
- Earthquake (5)
- Lithosphere (6)
- Earth's inner core (7)

</div>

1.  The theory that Earth's lithosphere is divided into large plates that move over the underlying mantle, shaping continents, oceans, and mountain belts. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics).
2.  The hypothesis that continents move relative to one another across the Earth's surface — the historical precursor to plate tectonics. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_drift).
3.  The process by which new oceanic crust forms as tectonic plates pull apart at mid-ocean ridges. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seafloor_spreading).
4.  The process in which one tectonic plate moves beneath another and sinks into the mantle at a convergent boundary. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction).
5.  Shaking of the Earth's surface caused by a sudden release of energy in the lithosphere, generating seismic waves. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake).
6.  The rigid outer shell of the Earth, comprising the crust and the uppermost mantle, that is broken into tectonic plates. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithosphere).
7.  The Earth's innermost layer: a solid ball composed primarily of iron and nickel, inferred from seismic-wave behavior. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_inner_core).

## Meteorology & Atmospheric Science

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Meteorology (1)
- Atmosphere of Earth (2)
- Weather (3)
- Jet stream (4)
- Coriolis effect (5)

</div>

1.  The scientific study of the atmosphere, focused especially on weather processes and forecasting. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorology).
2.  The layer of gases retained around the Earth by gravity, structured into layers such as the troposphere and stratosphere. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmosphere_of_Earth).
3.  The state of the atmosphere at a given place and time — temperature, humidity, wind, cloud cover, and precipitation. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather).
4.  A fast, narrow band of strong winds in the upper atmosphere that steers weather systems. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_stream).
5.  The apparent deflection of moving objects (such as air and ocean currents) caused by the Earth's rotation. **Aliases:** Coriolis force. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coriolis_force).

## Oceanography

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Oceanography (1)
- Ocean current (2)
- Thermohaline circulation (3)
- Salinity (4)

</div>

1.  The study of the physical, chemical, geological, and biological features of the ocean. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanography).
2.  A continuous, directed movement of seawater driven by forces such as wind, temperature, salinity, and the Earth's rotation. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_current).
3.  The large-scale, global ocean circulation driven by density differences arising from temperature (thermo) and salinity (haline). **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermohaline_circulation).
4.  The concentration of dissolved salts in a body of water. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinity).

## Climatology

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Climatology (1)
- Climate (2)
- Greenhouse effect (3)
- Milankovitch cycles (4)

</div>

1.  The scientific study of climate — weather conditions averaged over long periods — and of climate change. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climatology).
2.  The long-term average of weather patterns in a region, typically measured over decades. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate).
3.  The warming of a planet's surface caused by atmospheric gases that absorb and re-emit thermal radiation. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_effect).
4.  Cyclical variations in the Earth's orbit and axial tilt that alter the distribution of solar radiation and pace long-term climate change. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milankovitch_cycles).

## Hydrology

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Hydrology (1)
- Water cycle (2)
- Aquifer (3)
- Groundwater (4)

</div>

1.  The science of the movement, distribution, and management of water on and beneath the Earth's surface. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrology).
2.  The continuous circulation of water through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and runoff. **Aliases:** hydrologic cycle. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_cycle).
3.  An underground layer of permeable rock or sediment that holds and transmits groundwater. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquifer).
4.  Water held in the pore spaces and fractures of soil and rock below the surface. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundwater).
