# Astronomy

## Introduction

Astronomy is the natural science that studies celestial objects and phenomena — planets,
moons, stars, nebulae, galaxies, and the universe as a whole. It draws on mathematics,
physics, and chemistry to explain how these objects form, move, and evolve, spanning scales
from our own Solar System out to the large-scale structure of the cosmos. This page is a
**Map of Content**: newcomers can anchor on a few core ideas — orbits and gravity, stellar
evolution, redshift, and the expanding universe — then follow the links below into the
working vocabulary, the people behind it, and where to read further.

## Related concepts & fields

- **[Physics](../physics/index.en.md)** — supplies the gravity, thermodynamics, and nuclear
  physics that explain orbits, stellar energy, and the evolution of the universe; astrophysics
  sits at the boundary of the two fields.
- **[Earth Science](../earth_science/index.en.md)** — shares planetary science and methods
  for studying atmospheres, geology, and climate on Earth and other worlds.
- **[Mathematics](../../formal_sciences/mathematics/index.en.md)** — provides the geometry,
  calculus, and differential equations used to model orbits and celestial mechanics.
- **[Chemistry](../chemistry/index.en.md)** — underpins spectroscopy and the study of the
  composition of stars, nebulae, and planetary atmospheres.
