# Key Terms & Concepts — Chemistry

A curated, scannable reference of important terms in chemistry. Hover a term to reveal its
definition, aliases, and an external anchor. Terms are grouped by real sub-topics — never a
`General` or `Other` catch-all.

## Atomic Structure

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Atom (1)
- Proton (2)
- Neutron (3)
- Electron (4)
- Isotope (5)

</div>

1.  The basic particle of a chemical element, consisting of a nucleus of protons and neutrons surrounded by electrons. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom).
2.  A positively charged subatomic particle found in the atomic nucleus; its count defines the element. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton).
3.  An electrically neutral subatomic particle found in the atomic nucleus alongside protons. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron).
4.  A negatively charged subatomic particle occupying the region around the nucleus and governing chemical bonding. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron).
5.  One of two or more atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope).

## Chemical Bonding

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Chemical bond (1)
- Covalent bond (2)
- Ionic bond (3)
- Hydrogen bond (4)
- Electronegativity (5)

</div>

1.  A lasting attraction between atoms, ions, or molecules that enables the formation of chemical compounds. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_bond).
2.  A bond in which atoms share one or more pairs of electrons. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covalent_bond).
3.  A bond formed by the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. **Aliases:** electrovalent bond. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionic_bonding).
4.  A weak attraction between a hydrogen atom bonded to an electronegative atom and another electronegative atom nearby. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_bond).
5.  The tendency of an atom to attract a shared pair of electrons toward itself. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronegativity).

## The Periodic Table

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Periodic table (1)
- Chemical element (2)
- Atomic number (3)
- Valence electron (4)

</div>

1.  A tabular arrangement of the chemical elements ordered by atomic number and grouped by recurring chemical properties. **Aliases:** periodic table of the elements. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_table).
2.  A pure substance consisting of atoms that all have the same number of protons. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_element).
3.  The number of protons in an atom's nucleus, which uniquely identifies its element. **Aliases:** proton number, Z. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_number).
4.  An electron in the outermost shell of an atom that can participate in chemical bonding. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valence_electron).

## Reactions & Stoichiometry

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Chemical reaction (1)
- Stoichiometry (2)
- Mole (3)
- Catalysis (4)
- Redox (5)

</div>

1.  A process that transforms one set of chemical substances (reactants) into another (products). **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_reaction).
2.  The calculation of the quantitative relationships between reactants and products in a chemical reaction. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoichiometry).
3.  The SI base unit of amount of substance, defined as exactly 6.02214076×10²³ elementary entities. **Aliases:** mol. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mole_(unit)).
4.  The increase in the rate of a chemical reaction by adding a substance (a catalyst) that is not consumed by the reaction. **Aliases:** catalyst. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalysis).
5.  A type of chemical reaction in which the oxidation states of atoms change through the transfer of electrons. **Aliases:** oxidation–reduction. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redox).

## Thermochemistry

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Enthalpy (1)
- Entropy (2)
- Gibbs free energy (3)
- Activation energy (4)

</div>

1.  A thermodynamic quantity equal to a system's internal energy plus the product of its pressure and volume; its change measures heat absorbed or released at constant pressure. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enthalpy).
2.  A thermodynamic quantity measuring the degree of disorder or randomness in a system. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy).
3.  A thermodynamic potential whose change predicts whether a reaction at constant temperature and pressure is spontaneous. **Aliases:** Gibbs energy, free enthalpy. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbs_free_energy).
4.  The minimum energy that reacting species must possess for a chemical reaction to occur. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activation_energy).

## Acids & Bases

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Acid (1)
- Base (2)
- pH (3)

</div>

1.  A substance that donates protons or accepts electron pairs, typically forming a solution with a pH below 7. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid).
2.  A substance that accepts protons or donates electron pairs, typically forming a solution with a pH above 7. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_(chemistry)).
3.  A logarithmic scale used to specify the acidity or basicity of an aqueous solution. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PH).

## Organic Chemistry

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Organic chemistry (1)
- Hydrocarbon (2)
- Functional group (3)
- Isomer (4)
- Polymer (5)

</div>

1.  The branch of chemistry that studies the structure, properties, and reactions of carbon-containing compounds. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry).
2.  An organic compound consisting entirely of hydrogen and carbon. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon).
3.  A specific group of atoms within a molecule responsible for its characteristic chemical reactions. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_group).
4.  One of two or more compounds with the same molecular formula but a different arrangement of atoms. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomer).
5.  A substance composed of very large molecules built from many repeating subunits. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer).
