# Sources & Further Reading — Chemistry

A curated list of resources for diving deeper into chemistry.

## Websites & Online Publications

1. PubChem
    - *Link*: [pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/)
    - *Note*: A free chemistry database from the US National Institutes of Health, covering the structures, properties, and safety of millions of compounds.
2. Khan Academy — Chemistry
    - *Link*: [khanacademy.org/science/chemistry](https://www.khanacademy.org/science/chemistry)
    - *Note*: Free courses spanning atomic structure, bonding, stoichiometry, thermodynamics, and organic chemistry.
3. IUPAC
    - *Link*: [iupac.org](https://iupac.org/)
    - *Note*: The authority on chemical nomenclature and terminology, and the source of the official periodic table.

## Videos & Documentaries

1. Crash Course Chemistry
    - *Link*: [youtube.com/playlist?list=PL8dPuuaLjXtPHzzYuWy6fYEaX9mQQ8oGr](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL8dPuuaLjXtPHzzYuWy6fYEaX9mQQ8oGr)
    - *Note*: A fast-paced introductory series covering the core of a first chemistry course.
2. Periodic Videos
    - *Link*: [periodicvideos.com](https://www.periodicvideos.com/)
    - *Note*: A video for every element in the periodic table, produced by chemists at the University of Nottingham.

## Books

1. *Chemistry: The Central Science* — by Theodore L. Brown, H. Eugene LeMay, and Bruce E. Bursten
    - *Note*: A widely used general-chemistry textbook covering the standard undergraduate curriculum.
2. *The Disappearing Spoon* — by Sam Kean
    - *Note*: A popular-science tour of the periodic table told through the histories behind its elements.
3. *Napoleon's Buttons* — by Penny Le Couteur and Jay Burreson
    - *Note*: Seventeen molecules and the outsized roles they played in shaping human history.
