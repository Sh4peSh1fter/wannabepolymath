# Sources & Further Reading — Literature

A curated list of resources for diving deeper into literature.

## Websites & Online Publications

1. Poetry Foundation
    - *Link*: [poetryfoundation.org](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/)
    - *Note*: Publisher of *Poetry* magazine, with a large online archive of poems, poet biographies, and essays.
2. Project Gutenberg
    - *Link*: [gutenberg.org](https://www.gutenberg.org/)
    - *Note*: A free digital library of over 70,000 public-domain books, including much of the classic literary canon.

## Books

1. *Poetics* — by Aristotle
    - *Link*: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetics_(Aristotle))
    - *Note*: The earliest surviving work of dramatic theory and literary criticism.
2. *Anatomy of Criticism* — by Northrop Frye
    - *Link*: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomy_of_Criticism)
    - *Note*: A landmark 1957 work proposing a systematic theory of literary genres, modes, and archetypes.
3. *Mimesis: The Representation of Reality in Western Literature* — by Erich Auerbach
    - *Link*: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimesis_(Auerbach))
    - *Note*: A classic comparative study of realism across Western literature from Homer to the modern novel.
4. *A Glossary of Literary Terms* — by M. H. Abrams
    - *Link*: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Glossary_of_Literary_Terms)
    - *Note*: A standard, widely used reference defining the vocabulary of literary study.
5. *The Norton Anthology of English Literature*
    - *Link*: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Norton_Anthology_of_English_Literature)
    - *Note*: A comprehensive teaching anthology of English-language literature, a staple of university courses.
