# Key Terms & Concepts — Linguistics

A curated, scannable reference of important terms in linguistics. Hover a term to reveal its
definition, aliases, and an external anchor. Terms are grouped by real sub-fields — never a
`General` or `Other` catch-all.

## Phonetics & Phonology

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Phonetics (1)
- Phonology (2)
- Phoneme (3)
- Allophone (4)
- International Phonetic Alphabet (5)

</div>

1.  The study of the physical sounds of human speech — their articulation, acoustic properties, and perception. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonetics).
2.  The study of how sounds are organized and patterned within a particular language or across languages. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonology).
3.  The smallest unit of sound that can distinguish meaning between words in a language. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoneme).
4.  One of several phonetically distinct realizations of the same phoneme, typically occurring in complementary distribution. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allophone).
5.  A standardized system of symbols for representing the sounds of spoken language. **Aliases:** IPA. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet).

## Morphology

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Morphology (1)
- Morpheme (2)
- Affix (3)
- Inflection (4)

</div>

1.  The study of the internal structure of words and the rules by which they are formed. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphology_(linguistics)).
2.  The smallest grammatical unit in a language that carries meaning. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morpheme).
3.  A morpheme attached to a word stem to form a new word or word form, such as a prefix or suffix. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affix).
4.  The modification of a word to express grammatical features such as tense, number, case, or gender. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflection).

## Syntax

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Syntax (1)
- Grammar (2)
- Generative grammar (3)
- Universal grammar (4)
- Dependency grammar (5)

</div>

1.  The study of the rules governing how words combine to form phrases and sentences. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntax).
2.  The system of structural rules governing the composition of words, phrases, and sentences in a language. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammar).
3.  A theoretical framework, associated with Noam Chomsky, that models grammar as a finite set of rules able to generate all and only the grammatical sentences of a language. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_grammar).
4.  The hypothesis that the capacity for language is innate and that all human languages share a common underlying structure. **Aliases:** UG. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_grammar).
5.  A class of syntactic theories that describe sentence structure through directed relations (dependencies) between individual words rather than phrase constituents. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependency_grammar).

## Semantics & Pragmatics

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Semantics (1)
- Pragmatics (2)
- Deixis (3)
- Speech act (4)
- Semiotics (5)

</div>

1.  The study of meaning in language, including the meaning of words, phrases, and sentences. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantics).
2.  The study of how context contributes to meaning and how language is used in communication. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatics).
3.  The use of words whose reference depends on the context of utterance, such as "here," "now," or "you." **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deixis).
4.  An utterance that performs an action — such as promising, ordering, or apologizing — rather than merely stating a fact. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_act).
5.  The study of signs and symbols and their use or interpretation. **Aliases:** semiology. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiotics).

## Sociolinguistics

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Sociolinguistics (1)
- Dialect (2)
- Register (3)
- Code-switching (4)

</div>

1.  The study of the relationship between language and society, including how language varies across social groups and contexts. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociolinguistics).
2.  A variety of a language distinguished by features of vocabulary, grammar, and pronunciation associated with a region or social group. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect).
3.  A variety of language used for a particular purpose or social setting, varying chiefly by degree of formality. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Register_(sociolinguistics)).
4.  The practice of alternating between two or more languages or language varieties within a single conversation or utterance. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code-switching).

## Historical & Comparative Linguistics

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Historical linguistics (1)
- Comparative method (2)
- Proto-language (3)
- Language family (4)

</div>

1.  The study of how languages change over time and of the relationships between languages. **Aliases:** diachronic linguistics. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_linguistics).
2.  A technique for reconstructing ancestral languages by systematically comparing features of related languages. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_method).
3.  A hypothesized common ancestor from which a group of related languages is descended. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-language).
4.  A group of languages descended from a common ancestral proto-language. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_family).

## Language Acquisition & Psycholinguistics

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Psycholinguistics (1)
- Language acquisition (2)
- Second-language acquisition (3)

</div>

1.  The study of the psychological and neurobiological factors that enable humans to acquire, use, comprehend, and produce language. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psycholinguistics).
2.  The process by which humans acquire the capacity to perceive, produce, and use words to understand and communicate. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_acquisition).
3.  The process of learning a language in addition to one's native language, and the scholarly study of that process. **Aliases:** SLA. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-language_acquisition).
