# Linguistics

## Introduction

Linguistics is the scientific study of language. It analyzes language form (the sounds, words,
and sentences that make up a language), language meaning, and language in social context —
spanning phonetics and phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics and pragmatics,
sociolinguistics, and the historical study of how languages change and relate. This field is
organized in three views: the working **vocabulary**, the **people and institutions** behind
it, and where to **read further**.

## Related concepts & fields

- **[Philosophy](../philosophy/index.en.md)** — the philosophy of language, logic, and
  meaning shares deep questions with semantics, pragmatics, and semiotics.
- **[Computer Science](../../formal_sciences/computer_science/index.en.md)** — computational
  linguistics and natural-language processing apply formal grammars and models of syntax and
  semantics to language technology.
- **[Psychology](../../social_sciences/psychology/index.en.md)** — psycholinguistics and
  language acquisition study how the mind produces, comprehends, and learns language.
- **[Anthropology](../../social_sciences/anthropology/index.en.md)** — linguistic anthropology
  studies language as a cultural practice, complementing sociolinguistics.
