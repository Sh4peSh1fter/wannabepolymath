# Key Terms & Concepts — Philosophy

A curated, scannable reference of important terms in philosophy. Hover a term to reveal its
definition, aliases, and an external anchor. Terms are grouped by real sub-fields — never a
`General` or `Other` catch-all.

## Epistemology

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Epistemology (1)
- A priori and a posteriori (2)
- Empiricism (3)
- Rationalism (4)
- Skepticism (5)

</div>

1.  The branch of philosophy concerned with the nature, sources, and limits of knowledge. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology).
2.  A distinction between knowledge justified independently of experience (a priori) and knowledge dependent on empirical evidence (a posteriori). **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_priori_and_a_posteriori).
3.  The view that knowledge comes primarily or wholly from sensory experience. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empiricism).
4.  The view that reason, rather than experience, is the chief source and test of knowledge. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalism).
5.  A questioning attitude toward the possibility of certain or complete knowledge. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skepticism).

## Metaphysics

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Metaphysics (1)
- Ontology (2)
- Free will (3)
- Determinism (4)

</div>

1.  The branch of philosophy examining the fundamental nature of reality, being, and existence. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics).
2.  The study of being, existence, and the basic categories of what there is. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology).
3.  The capacity of agents to choose between genuinely possible courses of action. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_will).
4.  The view that all events, including human choices, are fully determined by previously existing causes. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Determinism).

## Ethics

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Ethics (1)
- Utilitarianism (2)
- Deontology (3)
- Virtue ethics (4)
- Categorical imperative (5)

</div>

1.  The branch of philosophy concerned with concepts of right and wrong conduct. **Aliases:** moral philosophy. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics).
2.  The ethical theory holding that the right action is the one that maximizes overall well-being or happiness. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utilitarianism).
3.  The ethical theory that judges the morality of actions by their adherence to rules or duties. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deontology).
4.  An approach to ethics emphasizing moral character and the virtues rather than rules or consequences. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtue_ethics).
5.  Kant's central moral principle: act only on maxims that you could consistently will to become universal laws. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_imperative).

## Logic

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Logic (1)
- Deductive reasoning (2)
- Inductive reasoning (3)
- Fallacy (4)

</div>

1.  The systematic study of valid inference and the principles of correct reasoning. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic).
2.  Inference in which the conclusion follows necessarily from the premises. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deductive_reasoning).
3.  Inference in which the premises provide probable but not conclusive support for the conclusion. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_reasoning).
4.  A flaw in reasoning that renders an argument invalid or unsound. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallacy).

## Philosophy of Mind

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Philosophy of mind (1)
- Consciousness (2)
- Mind–body dualism (3)
- Qualia (4)

</div>

1.  The branch of philosophy studying the nature of the mind and its relation to the body. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_mind).
2.  Subjective awareness of one's own existence, sensations, and thoughts. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness).
3.  The view that the mind and the body (or the mental and the physical) are distinct kinds of substance. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind%E2%80%93body_dualism).
4.  The subjective, qualitative properties of conscious experiences — "what it is like" to have them. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualia).

## Political Philosophy

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Political philosophy (1)
- Social contract (2)
- Justice (3)

</div>

1.  The study of government, justice, rights, and the legitimacy of political authority. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_philosophy).
2.  The theory that legitimate political authority derives from an agreement among the individuals who are governed. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_contract).
3.  The concept of fairness in the distribution of goods and the treatment of persons. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice).

## Aesthetics

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Aesthetics (1)
- Sublime (2)

</div>

1.  The branch of philosophy concerned with the nature of beauty, art, and taste. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesthetics).
2.  A quality of greatness or grandeur — in nature or art — that exceeds calculation or imitation. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sublime_(philosophy)).
