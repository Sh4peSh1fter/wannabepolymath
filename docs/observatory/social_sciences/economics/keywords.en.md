# Key Terms & Concepts — Economics

A curated, scannable reference of important terms in economics — spanning economic theory and
the practice of investing. Hover a term to reveal its definition, aliases, and an external
anchor. Terms are grouped by real sub-topics — never a `General` or `Other` catch-all.

## Microeconomics

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Supply and demand (1)
- Elasticity (2)
- Opportunity cost (3)
- Marginal utility (4)

</div>

1.  An economic model of price determination in a market: price and quantity settle where the amount buyers demand equals the amount sellers supply. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_and_demand).
2.  A measure of how strongly one economic variable responds to a change in another — for example, how much the quantity demanded of a good changes when its price changes. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_(economics)).
3.  The value of the next-best alternative that is given up when a choice is made. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_cost).
4.  The additional satisfaction a consumer gains from consuming one more unit of a good or service. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_utility).

## Macroeconomics

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Gross domestic product (1)
- Fiscal policy (2)
- Monetary policy (3)
- Unemployment (4)
- Inflation (5)

</div>

1.  The total market value of all final goods and services produced within a country over a given period. **Aliases:** GDP. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product).
2.  A government's use of taxation and public spending to influence the economy. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_policy).
3.  A central bank's management of interest rates and the money supply to pursue goals such as price stability and full employment. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetary_policy).
4.  The share of the labor force that is without work but available for and actively seeking employment. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment).
5.  A sustained rise in the general price level, which reduces the purchasing power of money. **Aliases:** אינפלציה. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation).

## Finance Fundamentals

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Compound interest (1)

</div>

1.  Interest calculated on both the initial principal and the accumulated interest from prior periods, so a balance grows exponentially over time. **Aliases:** ריבית דה ריבית. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_interest).

## Markets & Instruments

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Stock (share) (1)
- Stock exchange (2)
- Market index (3)
- Bond (4)
- Dividend (5)
- Ticker symbol (6)
- Credit rating (7)

</div>

1.  A security representing fractional ownership of a company. **Aliases:** מנייה, share. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock).
2.  An organized marketplace where securities such as stocks and bonds are bought and sold. **Aliases:** בורסה. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_exchange).
3.  A statistical measure tracking the combined performance of a group of securities, used as a benchmark for a market or segment. **Aliases:** מדד, stock market index. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market_index).
4.  A fixed-income instrument representing a loan from an investor to a borrower — typically a government or corporation — that pays interest over a set term. **Aliases:** אג״ח. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_(finance)).
5.  A distribution of a portion of a company's earnings to its shareholders. **Aliases:** דיבידנט. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dividend).
6.  A short, unique code identifying a publicly traded security on an exchange (e.g. the `ticker` `AAPL`). **Aliases:** ticker. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticker_symbol).
7.  An assessment of the creditworthiness of a borrower or a specific debt instrument, issued by credit-rating agencies. **Aliases:** דירוג אשראי. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_rating).

## Investment Vehicles

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Index-tracking ETF (1)
- Exchange-traded note (2)
- Pension (3)
- Keren Hishtalmut (4)
- Expense ratio (5)

</div>

1.  An exchange-traded fund built to replicate the performance of a market index, giving low-cost, diversified exposure to that index. **Aliases:** קרן סל מחקה מדד. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange-traded_fund).
2.  An exchange-traded product whose return follows a benchmark index; internationally it is a debt security issued by a bank. In Israel the equivalent "teudat sal" certificates were obligations of the issuer and were largely converted into ETFs (קרן סל) in a market reform. **Aliases:** תעודת סל. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange-traded_note).
3.  A fund into which contributions are paid during a person's working life to provide income in retirement. **Aliases:** פנסייה. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pension).
4.  An Israeli tax-advantaged savings fund originally intended for professional further education, now widely used as a medium-term savings and investment vehicle, with tax benefits on contributions and gains under Israeli law. **Aliases:** קרן השתלמות.
5.  The annual fee a fund charges its investors, expressed as a percentage of the assets it manages. **Aliases:** expense ratio. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expense_ratio).

## Trading

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Trading account (1)
- Broker (2)
- Order types (3)
- Bull market (4)
- Bear market (5)
- Stop-loss order (6)

</div>

1.  An account held with a bank or brokerage through which an investor buys and sells securities. In Israel these come in sub-kinds: bank accounts, accounts at investment houses (בתי השקעות), and non-bank trading platforms (פלטפורמות חוץ בנקאיות). **Aliases:** חשבון מסחר. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securities_account).
2.  A person or firm that arranges securities transactions on behalf of clients, usually for a fee or commission. **Aliases:** ברוקר. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broker).
3.  Instructions specifying how a trade should be executed: a market order fills immediately at the best available price, while a limit order fills only at a specified price or better. **Aliases:** שיטות מסחר. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_(exchange)).
4.  A period of sustained rising prices in a market, often accompanied by investor optimism. **Aliases:** שוק שורי. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_trend).
5.  A period of sustained falling prices in a market, commonly defined as a decline of 20% or more from recent highs. **Aliases:** שוק דובי. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_trend).
6.  An order to sell a security once it reaches a specified price, used to cap the loss on a position. **Aliases:** stop loss. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_(exchange)).
