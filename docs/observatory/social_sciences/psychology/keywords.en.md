## Cognitive Psychology

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- McGurk effect (1)
- Heuristics (2)
- Anchoring effect (3)

</div>

1.  A perceptual phenomenon demonstrating an interaction between hearing and vision in speech perception. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGurk_effect).
2.  Mental shortcuts or rules of thumb that simplify judgment and decision-making — often efficient, but sometimes the source of systematic biases. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heuristic_(psychology)).
3.  A cognitive bias whereby an initial piece of information (the "anchor") disproportionately influences subsequent judgments and estimates. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchoring_effect).

## Psychometrics

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Big Five personality traits (1)
- Rorschach test (2)
- Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale (3)
- Flynn effect (4)

</div>

1.  A model describing personality along five basic dimensions (openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism). **Aliases:** OCEAN, five-factor model. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Five_personality_traits).
2.  A projective test in which a person describes what they see in a series of inkblots. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rorschach_test).
3.  A widely used test measuring adult and adolescent intelligence, producing a full-scale IQ. **Aliases:** WAIS. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wechsler_Adult_Intelligence_Scale).
4.  The substantial, sustained rise in measured intelligence-test scores across generations during the 20th century. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flynn_effect).

## Judgment & Decision-Making

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Extraneous factors in judicial decisions (1)

</div>

1.  A widely cited finding that judges' rulings are influenced by irrelevant factors such as the time since their last break — popularly the "hungry judge effect." **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungry_judge_effect).

</div>

1.  A pseudoscientific approach to communication, personal development, and psychotherapy. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuro-linguistic_programming).
