# Psychology

## Introduction

Psychology is the scientific study of mind and behavior. It spans many sub-fields — from
**cognitive psychology** (how we perceive, remember, and reason) and **psychometrics** (how
mental traits like intelligence and personality are measured) to **judgment and
decision-making**, clinical practice, developmental, and social psychology. The discipline
sits between the natural and social sciences: it borrows experimental method and biology from
one side and questions of society, culture, and meaning from the other. This page is a **Map
of Content** — it orients and links; the definitions live in `keywords`, the people in
`entities`, and the reading in `sources`.

## Related concepts & fields

- **[Sociology](../sociology/index.en.md)** — shares the study of social behavior; social
  psychology sits at the boundary between individual mind and group dynamics.
- **[Anthropology](../anthropology/index.en.md)** — both examine human behavior, but
  anthropology foregrounds culture and cross-cultural variation.
- **[Biology](../../natural_sciences/biology/index.en.md)** — the substrate of mind;
  neuroscience and behavioral genetics ground psychology in the brain and the body.
- **[Philosophy](../../humanities/philosophy/index.en.md)** — the origin of psychology's core
  questions about consciousness, perception, and the nature of mind.
