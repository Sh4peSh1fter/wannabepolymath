# Anthropology

## Introduction

Anthropology is the scientific study of humanity, concerned with human behavior, biology,
cultures, societies, and language, in both the present and the past. It is distinctive for its
holistic and comparative outlook, traditionally organized into four subfields — cultural,
biological, archaeological, and linguistic anthropology — and for its signature method of
long-term, firsthand fieldwork (ethnography). This page is a Map of Content: the working
**vocabulary**, the **people and institutions** behind the field, and where to **read further**.

## Related concepts & fields

- **[Sociology](../sociology/index.en.md)** — a sister social science; both study human groups
  and social organization, differing in emphasis and traditional method (fieldwork versus survey).
- **[History](../history/index.en.md)** — shares an interest in past human societies, which
  archaeology reconstructs from material remains where written records are absent.
- **[Linguistics](../../humanities/linguistics/index.en.md)** — overlaps directly with
  linguistic anthropology on how language shapes and reflects culture and cognition.
- **[Biology](../../natural_sciences/biology/index.en.md)** — grounds biological anthropology,
  which studies human evolution, primates, and the human fossil record.
