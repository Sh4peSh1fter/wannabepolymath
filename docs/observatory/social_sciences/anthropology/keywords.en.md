# Key Terms & Concepts — Anthropology

A curated, scannable reference of important terms in anthropology. Hover a term to reveal its
definition, aliases, and an external anchor. Terms are grouped by real sub-fields — never a
`General` or `Other` catch-all.

## Cultural Anthropology

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Culture (1)
- Cultural relativism (2)
- Ethnocentrism (3)
- Emic and etic (4)
- Enculturation (5)

</div>

1.  The system of shared beliefs, values, customs, behaviors, and artifacts that members of a society learn and use to interact with the world and one another. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture).
2.  The principle that a person's beliefs and practices should be understood on the basis of that person's own culture rather than judged against the criteria of another. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_relativism).
3.  The tendency to evaluate other cultures according to the standards and customs of one's own. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnocentrism).
4.  Two contrasting approaches to describing behavior — emic from within the culture's own conceptual framework, etic from the external framework of the observer. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emic_and_etic).
5.  The process by which a person learns the norms, values, and behaviors appropriate to their culture. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enculturation).

## Biological (Physical) Anthropology

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Biological anthropology (1)
- Human evolution (2)
- Paleoanthropology (3)
- Primatology (4)
- Hominini (5)

</div>

1.  The subfield studying the biological and behavioral aspects of humans, their extinct ancestors, and related non-human primates. **Aliases:** physical anthropology. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_anthropology).
2.  The evolutionary process that led to the emergence of anatomically modern humans. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_evolution).
3.  The study of ancient humans and human ancestors through fossil and other physical remains. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleoanthropology).
4.  The scientific study of non-human primates. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primatology).
5.  The taxonomic tribe that includes modern humans together with their extinct bipedal relatives. **Aliases:** hominins. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominini).

## Archaeology

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Archaeology (1)
- Excavation (2)
- Stratigraphy (3)
- Artifact (4)
- Radiocarbon dating (5)

</div>

1.  The study of human activity through the recovery and analysis of material culture. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology).
2.  The controlled recovery and recording of buried archaeological remains from the ground. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excavation_(archaeology)).
3.  The study of layered deposits (strata) to establish the relative chronological sequence of a site. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratigraphy_(archaeology)).
4.  An object made or modified by humans and recovered archaeologically, typically of cultural or historical interest. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artifact_(archaeology)).
5.  A method for determining the age of organic material by measuring its content of the radioactive isotope carbon-14. **Aliases:** carbon dating, carbon-14 dating. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiocarbon_dating).

## Linguistic Anthropology

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Linguistic anthropology (1)
- Linguistic relativity (2)
- Language documentation (3)

</div>

1.  The subfield studying how language shapes and reflects social life and culture. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_anthropology).
2.  The hypothesis that the structure of a language influences its speakers' worldview or cognition. **Aliases:** Sapir–Whorf hypothesis, Whorfianism. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_relativity).
3.  The systematic recording, analysis, and archiving of a language, especially an endangered one. **Aliases:** documentary linguistics. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_documentation).

## Ethnography & Fieldwork

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Ethnography (1)
- Participant observation (2)
- Field research (3)
- Thick description (4)
- Reflexivity (5)

</div>

1.  The systematic study and written description of individual cultures based on firsthand fieldwork. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnography).
2.  A fieldwork method in which the researcher takes part in the daily life of the group being studied while observing it. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Participant_observation).
3.  Data collection carried out in a natural, real-world setting rather than in a laboratory. **Aliases:** fieldwork. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_research).
4.  An account of human behavior that explains not only the behavior but its context, so that it becomes meaningful to an outsider. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thick_description).
5.  The practice of examining how the researcher's own position and actions affect the research process and its findings. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflexivity_(social_theory)).

## Kinship & Social Organization

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Kinship (1)
- Lineage (2)
- Clan (3)
- Exogamy (4)
- Rite of passage (5)

</div>

1.  The web of social relationships based on descent, marriage, and adoption that forms a central organizing principle in many societies. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinship).
2.  A kin group whose members trace descent from a common ancestor through a known genealogical line. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lineage_(anthropology)).
3.  A group of people united by perceived kinship and descent, often from a common ancestor. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clan).
4.  The social norm or custom of marrying outside one's own social group. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exogamy).
5.  A ritual marking a person's transition from one social status to another. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rite_of_passage).
