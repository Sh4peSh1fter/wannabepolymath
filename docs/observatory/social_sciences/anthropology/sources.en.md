# Sources & Further Reading — Anthropology

A curated list of resources for diving deeper into anthropology.

## Websites & Online Publications

1. American Anthropological Association
    - *Link*: [americananthro.org](https://www.americananthro.org/)
    - *Note*: The discipline's largest professional body, with resources, journals, and career and ethics guidance.
2. Encyclopaedia Britannica — Anthropology
    - *Link*: [britannica.com/science/anthropology](https://www.britannica.com/science/anthropology)
    - *Note*: A reliable, well-sourced overview of the field and its subfields.
3. Human Relations Area Files (HRAF)
    - *Link*: [hraf.yale.edu](https://hraf.yale.edu/)
    - *Note*: A Yale-based research agency behind eHRAF World Cultures, a database for cross-cultural comparison.

## Books

1. *Argonauts of the Western Pacific* — by Bronisław Malinowski
    - *Note*: A foundational ethnographic monograph that helped establish participant-observation fieldwork.
2. *Coming of Age in Samoa* — by Margaret Mead
    - *Note*: A widely read early ethnography of adolescence and culture.
3. *The Interpretation of Cultures* — by Clifford Geertz
    - *Note*: Essays that introduced interpretive anthropology and the idea of thick description.
4. *Patterns of Culture* — by Ruth Benedict
    - *Note*: An influential comparative account of how cultures form integrated wholes.
5. *Tristes Tropiques* — by Claude Lévi-Strauss
    - *Note*: A reflective memoir on fieldwork and the nature of anthropological inquiry.
