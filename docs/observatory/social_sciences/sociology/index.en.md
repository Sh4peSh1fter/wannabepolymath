# Sociology

## Introduction

Sociology is the systematic study of social life, social change, and the social causes and
consequences of human behavior. It ranges from the macro scale of institutions, classes, and
whole societies down to the micro scale of face-to-face interaction and the meanings people
attach to it. Its major theoretical traditions — structural functionalism, conflict theory,
and symbolic interactionism — offer competing lenses on the same social world, while its
research methods span the quantitative and the qualitative. This page is a Map of Content:
the definitions live in **keywords**, the people and organizations in **entities**, and the
reading in **sources**.

## Related concepts & fields

- **[Anthropology](../anthropology/index.en.md)** — shares an interest in culture, kinship,
  and social organization; sociology tends toward modern, large-scale societies while
  anthropology emphasizes comparative and ethnographic study.
- **[Psychology](../psychology/index.en.md)** — the two meet in social psychology, but
  sociology explains behavior through group structures and institutions rather than the
  individual mind.
- **[Political Science](../political_science/index.en.md)** — overlaps on power, authority,
  the state, and social movements.
- **[Economics](../economics/index.en.md)** — shares questions of class, inequality, and
  markets; economic sociology studies how social relations shape economic behavior.
