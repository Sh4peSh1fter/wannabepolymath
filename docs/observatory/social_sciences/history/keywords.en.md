# Key Terms & Concepts — History

A curated, scannable reference of important terms in history. Hover a term to reveal its
definition and an external anchor. Terms are grouped by real sub-topics — never a
`General` or `Other` catch-all.

## Historiography

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Historiography (1)
- Annales school (2)
- Longue durée (3)
- Whig history (4)
- Historical revisionism (5)

</div>

1.  The study of the methods, sources, and interpretive frameworks historians use — in effect, the history of historical writing itself. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiography).
2.  A 20th-century French school of history that emphasized long-term social and economic structures over political events. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annales_school).
3.  An approach, associated with the Annales school, that studies history across very long time spans rather than short-term events. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longue_dur%C3%A9e).
4.  An approach that presents the past as an inevitable progression toward liberty and enlightenment, often criticized as anachronistic. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whig_history).
5.  The reinterpretation of an established historical account, whether through legitimate new evidence or through illegitimate denial. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_revisionism).

## Historical Methods

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Historical method (1)
- Primary source (2)
- Secondary source (3)
- Source criticism (4)
- Palaeography (5)
- Prosopography (6)

</div>

1.  The body of techniques and guidelines historians use to research and write history from sources. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_method).
2.  An artifact, document, or record created at the time under study, offering direct evidence about it. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_source).
3.  An account that discusses, interprets, or synthesizes information drawn from primary sources. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_source).
4.  The process of evaluating a source's origin, reliability, and bias to assess its evidentiary value. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_criticism).
5.  The study of historical handwriting, used to read, date, and localize manuscripts. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeography).
6.  The investigation of a historical group through the collective study of its members' lives and relationships. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosopography).

## Periodization

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Periodization (1)
- Three-age system (2)
- Anno Domini (3)

</div>

1.  The division of the past into named, bounded blocks of time to make it easier to analyze. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodization).
2.  A scheme dividing prehistory into the Stone, Bronze, and Iron Ages by dominant tool-making material. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-age_system).
3.  The calendar era counting years from the traditional date of the birth of Jesus. **Aliases:** AD, Common Era (CE). **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anno_Domini).

## Ancient History

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Ancient history (1)
- Classical antiquity (2)
- Bronze Age (3)
- Hellenistic period (4)

</div>

1.  The span of recorded human history from the earliest writing systems to the early Middle Ages. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history).
2.  The period of interwoven Greek and Roman civilizations centered on the Mediterranean. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity).
3.  A prehistoric and early-historic period defined by the use of bronze for tools and weapons. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age).
4.  The era between the death of Alexander the Great and the rise of Rome, marked by the spread of Greek culture. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period).

## Medieval History

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Middle Ages (1)
- Feudalism (2)

</div>

1.  The period of European history from roughly the 5th to the late 15th century, between antiquity and the modern era. **Aliases:** medieval period. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages).
2.  A set of legal and military customs in medieval Europe structuring society around land held in exchange for service. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism).

## Modern History

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Renaissance (1)
- Early modern period (2)
- Age of Enlightenment (3)
- Industrial Revolution (4)

</div>

1.  A period of cultural, artistic, and intellectual revival in Europe bridging the Middle Ages and modernity. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance).
2.  The era from roughly 1500 to 1800, spanning the Renaissance, the Reformation, and the rise of nation-states. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period).
3.  An 18th-century intellectual movement emphasizing reason, science, and individual liberty. **Aliases:** the Enlightenment. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment).
4.  The transition to new manufacturing processes beginning in Britain around 1760, reshaping economy and society. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution).

## Japanese History

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Sengoku period (1)

</div>

1.  A period in Japanese history of nearly constant civil war, social upheaval, and political intrigue from 1467 to 1615. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sengoku_period).
