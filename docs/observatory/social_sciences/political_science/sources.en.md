# Sources & Further Reading — Political Science

A curated list of resources for diving deeper into political science.

## Websites & Online Publications

1. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy
    - *Link*: [plato.stanford.edu](https://plato.stanford.edu/)
    - *Note*: Peer-reviewed reference with authoritative entries on political philosophy, from the social contract to theories of justice.
2. Our World in Data — Democracy
    - *Link*: [ourworldindata.org/democracy](https://ourworldindata.org/democracy)
    - *Note*: Data-driven overview of how political regimes and democracy have changed across the world over time.

## Videos & Documentaries

1. CrashCourse
    - *Link*: [youtube.com/@crashcourse](https://www.youtube.com/@crashcourse)
    - *Note*: Hosts the "Government and Politics" and "Political Theory" series, accessible introductions to core concepts.

## Academic Papers & Journals

1. American Political Science Review
    - *Link*: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Political_Science_Review)
    - *Note*: The flagship peer-reviewed journal of the American Political Science Association, covering all major subfields.

## Books

1. *Politics* — by Aristotle
    - *Note*: The founding work of political science, analyzing the state, citizenship, and the forms of government.
2. *The Prince* — by Niccolò Machiavelli
    - *Note*: A foundational text of political realism on how power is acquired and held.
3. *Leviathan* — by Thomas Hobbes
    - *Note*: A cornerstone of social-contract theory arguing for a strong sovereign to escape the "state of nature."
4. *The Social Contract* — by Jean-Jacques Rousseau
    - *Note*: Develops the concepts of popular sovereignty and the general will that shaped democratic thought.
