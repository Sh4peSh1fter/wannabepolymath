# Key Terms & Concepts — Political Science

A curated, scannable reference of important terms in political science. Hover a term to reveal
its definition, aliases, and an external anchor. Terms are grouped by real sub-fields — never a
`General` or `Other` catch-all.

## Political Theory

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Sovereignty (1)
- Social contract (2)
- Legitimacy (3)
- Separation of powers (4)
- Rule of law (5)

</div>

1.  The supreme authority of a state to govern itself and its territory without external interference. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereignty).
2.  The idea that a person's moral and political obligations depend on an agreement among individuals to form the society in which they live. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_contract).
3.  The popular acceptance and recognition of an authority, institution, or ruling regime as rightful. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legitimacy_(political)).
4.  A model that divides the state's powers into separate legislative, executive, and judicial branches to prevent any one from concentrating power. **Aliases:** trias politica. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers).
5.  The principle that all persons, institutions, and the government itself are accountable to laws that are equally enforced and independently adjudicated. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_law).

## Political Ideologies

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Liberalism (1)
- Conservatism (2)
- Socialism (3)
- Nationalism (4)
- Fascism (5)

</div>

1.  A political and moral philosophy based on the rights of the individual, liberty, consent of the governed, political equality, and equality before the law. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism).
2.  A political and social philosophy promoting traditional social institutions and continuity, and favouring gradual change over radical reform. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism).
3.  A range of economic and social systems characterized by social ownership of the means of production, as opposed to private ownership. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism).
4.  An ideology holding that the nation should be congruent with the state and that loyalty to the nation surpasses other individual or group interests. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism).
5.  A far-right, authoritarian, ultranationalist ideology characterized by a dictatorial leader, centralized autocracy, militarism, forcible suppression of opposition, and strong regimentation of society and the economy. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism).

## Comparative Politics

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Democracy (1)
- Authoritarianism (2)
- Federalism (3)
- Apartheid (4)

</div>

1.  A system of government in which the population holds power, exercised directly or through freely elected representatives. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy).
2.  A form of government characterized by strong central power and limited political freedoms, with individual rights subordinate to the state. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism).
3.  A mode of political organization that unites separate states or regions under a central government while allowing each to retain its own affairs. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism).
4.  A system of institutionalized racial segregation that existed in South Africa and South West Africa from 1948 until the early 1990s. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid).

## International Relations

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Realism (1)
- Diplomacy (2)
- Balance of power (3)
- Nation state (4)
- Hegemony (5)

</div>

1.  A school of thought in international relations that emphasizes the competitive and conflictual role of states pursuing power and self-interest. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realism_(international_relations)).
2.  The practice of conducting negotiations and maintaining relations between states through official representatives. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomacy).
3.  A condition in which power is distributed among states so that no single state can dominate the others. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_of_power_(international_relations)).
4.  A political unit in which the state and the nation are congruent — a sovereign state whose citizens share a common identity. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_state).
5.  The political, economic, or military predominance of one state or group over others. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemony).

## Public Policy & Administration

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Public policy (1)
- Bureaucracy (2)
- Welfare state (3)

</div>

1.  The set of laws, regulatory measures, courses of action, and funding priorities enacted by a government on a given topic. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_policy).
2.  A body of non-elected officials and an administrative system governed by rules and hierarchical procedures, used to carry out the functions of a state or organization. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureaucracy).
3.  A form of government in which the state protects and promotes the economic and social well-being of its citizens. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state).

## Political Economy

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Political economy (1)
- Capitalism (2)

</div>

1.  The study of how political forces and economic systems interact, especially the production and distribution of wealth under different institutions. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_economy).
2.  An economic system based on private ownership of the means of production and their operation for profit. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism).
