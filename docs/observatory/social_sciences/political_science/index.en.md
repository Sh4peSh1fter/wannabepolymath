# Political Science

## Introduction

Political science is the scientific study of politics — of power, governance, and collective
decision-making. It asks how authority is justified (political theory), how regimes and
institutions differ across countries (comparative politics), how states interact in an
anarchic world (international relations), and how governments turn choices into action (public
policy). This page is a **Map of Content**: it orients and links, while the definitions live in
`keywords`, the people in `entities`, and the reading in `sources`.

## Related concepts & fields

- **[Sociology](../sociology/index.en.md)** — shares the study of institutions, power, and
  collective behavior; the state is a central object for both.
- **[Economics](../economics/index.en.md)** — meets political science in political economy,
  where markets and public policy shape one another.
- **[History](../history/index.en.md)** — supplies the record of regimes, revolutions, and
  wars that comparative politics and international relations interpret.
- **[Philosophy](../../humanities/philosophy/index.en.md)** — political theory grows directly
  out of moral and political philosophy (the social contract, justice, legitimacy).
