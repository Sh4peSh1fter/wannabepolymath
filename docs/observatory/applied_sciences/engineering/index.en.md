# Engineering

## Introduction

Engineering is the application of scientific and mathematical principles to design, build, and
maintain machines, structures, materials, and processes. It turns the abstract results of
physics, chemistry, and mathematics into working technology — bridges and buildings, circuits
and engines, chemical plants and control systems — always under real-world constraints of cost,
safety, and reliability. The discipline spans many specialized branches, from mechanical and
electrical to civil, chemical, and materials engineering. This field is organized in three
views: the working **vocabulary**, the **people and institutions** behind it, and where to
**read further**.

## Related concepts & fields

- **[Physics](../../natural_sciences/physics/index.en.md)** — engineering applies physics
  directly: mechanics and thermodynamics underpin mechanical design, and electromagnetism
  underpins electrical engineering.
- **[Mathematics](../../formal_sciences/mathematics/index.en.md)** — calculus, differential
  equations, and linear algebra are the analytical tools behind stress analysis, control
  theory, and the finite element method.
- **[Computer Science](../../formal_sciences/computer_science/index.en.md)** — computation
  drives modern engineering through CAD, simulation, embedded control systems, and digital
  signal processing.
