# Key Terms & Concepts — Medicine

A curated, scannable reference of important terms in medicine. Hover a term to reveal its
definition, aliases, and an external anchor. Terms are grouped by real sub-fields — never a
`General` or `Other` catch-all.

## Anatomy & Physiology

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Anatomy (1)
- Physiology (2)
- Homeostasis (3)
- Immune system (4)

</div>

1.  The branch of biology concerned with the structure of organisms and their parts. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomy).
2.  The scientific study of the functions and mechanisms of living systems. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology).
3.  The self-regulating process by which an organism maintains stable internal conditions despite changes in its environment. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeostasis).
4.  The network of biological processes and structures that protects an organism against disease. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_system).

## Pathology & Disease

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Pathology (1)
- Etiology (2)
- Inflammation (3)
- Fibromyalgia (4)

</div>

1.  The study of the causes and effects of disease, especially through laboratory examination of tissues, organs, and bodily fluids. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathology).
2.  The study of the causes or origins of a disease or condition. **Aliases:** aetiology. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etiology).
3.  A protective response of body tissue to injury or infection, characterized by redness, heat, swelling, and pain. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflammation).
4.  A long-term condition characterized by widespread musculoskeletal pain, fatigue, and heightened tenderness. **Aliases:** פיברומיאלגיה. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibromyalgia).

## Pharmacology

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Pharmacology (1)
- Pharmacokinetics (2)
- Analgesic (3)
- Antibiotic (4)
- Psilocybin (5)

</div>

1.  The branch of medicine concerned with the uses, effects, and modes of action of drugs. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacology).
2.  The study of how a drug is absorbed, distributed, metabolized, and excreted by the body over time. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacokinetics).
3.  A drug used to relieve pain. **Aliases:** painkiller. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analgesic).
4.  A type of antimicrobial substance active against bacteria, used to treat and prevent bacterial infections. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibiotic).
5.  A naturally occurring psychedelic compound produced by certain mushrooms, studied for potential treatment of depression and other psychiatric conditions. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psilocybin).

## Diagnostics

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Diagnosis (1)
- Prognosis (2)
- Biopsy (3)
- Magnetic resonance imaging (4)
- Functional health lab tests (5)

</div>

1.  The identification of a disease or condition from its signs and symptoms. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_diagnosis).
2.  A forecast of the likely course and outcome of a disease. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prognosis).
3.  A medical procedure in which a sample of tissue is removed for examination to determine the presence or extent of disease. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biopsy).
4.  A medical imaging technique that uses strong magnetic fields and radio waves to form detailed images of the body's internal structures. **Aliases:** MRI. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging).
5.  Tests that can help identify the root causes of disease and dysfunction in the body.

## Specialties

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Cardiology (1)
- Oncology (2)
- Neurology (3)
- Surgery (4)

</div>

1.  The branch of medicine dealing with disorders of the heart and the cardiovascular system. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiology).
2.  The branch of medicine that deals with the prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of cancer. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncology).
3.  The branch of medicine dealing with disorders of the nervous system. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurology).
4.  A medical specialty that uses operative, manual, and instrumental techniques to treat injury or disease. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surgery).

## Public Health

<div class="annotate" markdown>

- Epidemiology (1)
- Vaccine (2)
- Public health (3)

</div>

1.  The study of the distribution and determinants of health and disease within populations. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemiology).
2.  A biological preparation that provides active acquired immunity to a particular infectious disease. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccine).
3.  The science and practice of protecting and improving the health of people and communities. **See also:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health).
