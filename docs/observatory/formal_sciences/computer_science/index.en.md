# Computer Science

## Introduction

Computer Science is the study of computation, information, and automation — from the
theoretical limits of what can be computed to the practical engineering of software and
systems. Where computer *engineers* focus on hardware, computer *scientists* work mostly
with software: its theory, design, algorithms, and application. The field spans low-level
operating systems, networking, cryptography and security, programming languages, software
tooling, data science and machine learning, web and cloud platforms, and the storage and
processing of data at scale. This page is a map: the definitions live in **keywords**, the
people and organizations in **entities**, and where to read further in **sources**.

## Related concepts & fields

- **[Mathematics](../mathematics/index.en.md)** — supplies the algorithms, graph theory,
  probability, and number theory that computer science builds on; the two are deeply
  intertwined.
- **[Logic](../logic/index.en.md)** — the formal foundation of computation, proof, and the
  boundary between what is decidable and what is not.
- **[Engineering](../../applied_sciences/engineering/index.en.md)** — shares the design and
  systems-building practices that turn computing theory into working hardware and software.
- **Cryptography** — security and cryptographic protocols appear throughout this field; see
  the [cross-cutting map](../../cross_cutting/cryptography.en.md) for how the topic connects
  mathematics, computer science, and security.
