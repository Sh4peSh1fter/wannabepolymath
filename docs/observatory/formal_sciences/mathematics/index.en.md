# Mathematics

## Introduction

Mathematics is the study of quantity, structure, space, and change. It is the language the
other formal and natural sciences are written in — from the differential equations of physics
to the graph algorithms of computer science and the probability underpinning economics. This
field is organized in three views: the working **vocabulary**, the **people and institutions**
behind it, and where to **read further**.

## In this field

- [Key Terms & Concepts](./keywords.en.md) — the working vocabulary, grouped by sub-field
  (number theory, algebra, analysis, geometry, graph theory, algorithms, and more).
- [Key Entities](./entities.en.md) — the mathematicians, societies, and institutes that
  shaped the field.
- [Sources & Further Reading](./sources.en.md) — references, courses, and books.

## Related concepts & fields

- **[Computer Science](../computer_science/index.en.md)** — shares algorithms, complexity
  (P versus NP), graph theory, and cryptography (RSA); the two fields are deeply intertwined.
- **[Logic](../logic/index.en.md)** — the formal foundations of proof and the boundary
  between what is provable and what is computable.
- **[Physics](../../natural_sciences/physics/index.en.md)** — the primary consumer of
  calculus, differential equations, and linear algebra.
- **Cryptography** — number theory (RSA, the Chinese Remainder Theorem, Euler's totient
  function) is the mathematical foundation of modern cryptography; see the
  [cross-cutting map](../../cross_cutting/cryptography.en.md).
