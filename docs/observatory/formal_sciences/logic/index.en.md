# Logic

## Introduction

Logic is the systematic study of valid inference — the rules by which a conclusion may be
drawn from premises. It ranges from the truth-functional core of **propositional logic**,
through the quantified reasoning of **predicate logic**, to the **metalogical** results that
map the outer limits of proof and computation. Along the way it supplies the notions of
soundness, completeness, and decidability that let us judge arguments and formal systems
themselves. This page is a Map of Content: the definitions live in **keywords**, the people
in **entities**, and the reading in **sources**.

## Related concepts & fields

- **[Mathematics](../mathematics/index.en.md)** — logic supplies the axioms, proof, and set
  theory on which mathematics is founded, and studies the boundary between what is provable
  and what is computable.
- **[Computer Science](../computer_science/index.en.md)** — Boolean algebra, the Turing
  machine, decidability, and the halting problem originate in logic and underpin computation.
- **[Philosophy](../../humanities/philosophy/index.en.md)** — logic began as a branch of
  philosophy and remains its core tool for analysing arguments and detecting fallacies.
- **[Linguistics](../../humanities/linguistics/index.en.md)** — formal semantics uses
  predicate and modal logic to model the meaning of natural-language sentences.
